Hiring a social media marketing agency is one of the most common first marketing decisions a small business makes, and one of the most common to regret. The usual story is a monthly fee, a calendar of nicely designed posts, a report full of reach and impressions, and no clear change in the number of customers walking in.

It does not have to go that way. The difference is almost always in what you ask for before you sign.

## What a good social media agency does

At minimum, an agency working for a small business should:

1. Learn who your customers are and why they buy from you, before making anything.
2. Agree a small number of goals that connect to sales, such as enquiries, bookings or store visits.
3. Make content, mostly short video, that shows your product, your people and your customers.
4. Post consistently on the one or two platforms that matter for your business.
5. Reply to comments and messages quickly, or set up a process for your team to do it.
6. Run and optimise paid ads, if they are part of the plan.
7. Report every month on results against the goals, and say what they will change.

Point three is where most of the cost and most of the value sit. Design templates with your logo on stock photos are cheap to produce and rarely move anyone to buy.

## What it usually costs

For a small business in India, social media management commonly falls in these bands:

| Package | What it usually includes | Rough monthly fee |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | 8 to 12 posts on one platform, captions, scheduling | ₹8,000 to ₹20,000 |
| Standard | 12 to 20 posts including short videos, two platforms, community replies | ₹20,000 to ₹50,000 |
| Growth | Regular video shoots, paid ads management, monthly strategy call | ₹50,000 and up, plus ad spend |

Ad spend is usually separate from the fee. Make sure the ad money is paid directly from your own ad account, so you can see every rupee spent.

## Questions to ask before you sign

- Which of your current clients is most like my business, and what changed for them in the first six months?
- What will you need from me each month, in time, photos, access and approvals?
- Who will make the videos, and will you come to the shop or office to shoot?
- Which numbers will you report, and how will you track enquiries back to posts and ads?
- Will the ad account and the social accounts stay in my name?
- What is the notice period, and what do I get to keep if we stop?

## Contract terms worth pushing back on

- A long lock-in, such as twelve months, with no exit for poor results. Three months is plenty to judge progress, followed by month-to-month.
- Ad accounts or pages created in the agency's name. You should always own your accounts and be the admin.
- A fee calculated as a percentage of ad spend with no cap. It rewards spending more, whether or not it works.
- Reports that only show reach, impressions and followers.

## Setting realistic expectations

Organic social media grows slowly. For most small businesses, the first three months are about finding which content works and building a steady routine. Paid ads can produce enquiries within weeks, provided the offer is good and the landing page makes it easy to get in touch.

A good agency will tell you this upfront and set goals for month three and month six, not promise viral posts in week one.

## Doing it in-house instead

If someone in your team enjoys it, a sensible middle path is to handle day-to-day posting and replies yourself and pay a specialist for the parts that need skill: planning, a monthly video shoot, and paid ads. That often costs less than a full retainer and keeps the voice authentic.

Whichever way you go, decide the numbers you care about first, and judge the work by them every month. We report to our own marketing clients on enquiries, cost per enquiry and revenue first, with reach as the supporting detail.
